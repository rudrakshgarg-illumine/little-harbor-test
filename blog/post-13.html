Cozy Indoor Activities for Chicago Winter Days

January 20, 2020

When the wind comes off the lake and the sidewalks turn to ice, it can feel like winter lasts forever. Our teachers spend a lot of time indoors with busy little bodies this season, so we asked them for their favorite ideas to try at home.

Build a blanket fort

Chairs, sheets, couch cushions, and a flashlight are all you need. Fort building involves problem-solving, teamwork, and a lot of giggling. Once it is built, move story time inside.

Indoor snowball toss

Crumple white paper or use rolled-up socks as "snowballs." Set out a laundry basket and take turns tossing from different distances. Count how many make it in, and celebrate every attempt.

Ice painting

Freeze water in a baking dish, then set it on a towel. Children can paint on the ice with watercolors or drip colored water and watch it spread. It is beautiful, a little bit magical, and easy to clean up.

Kitchen helpers

Stirring, pouring, and measuring are wonderful for fine motor skills and early math. Even a simple snack, like spreading something on crackers, makes children feel capable and proud.

Dance party breaks

Put on a favorite song and freeze when the music stops. Movement games help children release energy and practice self-control at the same time.

Window watching

Look outside together and talk about what you see: birds at the feeder, snowflakes, people bundled in coats. Ask questions like "Where do you think that bird sleeps?" Simple conversations like these build vocabulary and curiosity.

Inside our centers, you will find children doing many of these same activities when outdoor play is not possible. Winter does not have to be a season of waiting. With a little creativity, it can be one of the coziest, most connected times of the year.
