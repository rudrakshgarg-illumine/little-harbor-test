The Kindness Tree

February 14, 2023

In one of our preschool classrooms this winter, a bare paper tree went up on the wall. By the end of the month, it was covered in bright paper hearts, and every one of them told a story.

The idea started after a hard week. A few children had been struggling with sharing, and there were more tears over toys than usual. Rather than focusing only on the conflicts, the teachers decided to help the class notice the good things happening around them.

Here is how it worked: whenever someone saw a classmate do something kind, they could tell a teacher, and together they wrote it on a paper heart for the tree. At first, the children needed a little help noticing. Soon, they were spotting kindness everywhere.

Some of the hearts said:

  • "She helped me zip my coat."
  • "He gave me the red crayon even though he wanted it."
  • "They let me be the dog in the game."
  • "My friend sat with me when I was sad."

Something lovely happened as the tree filled up. Children started being kind on purpose, not to earn a heart but because they felt proud of themselves. One child shared a favorite toy and then whispered to a teacher, "You don't have to write it down. I just wanted to."

Kindness is a skill, just like counting or climbing. Children learn it by seeing it, practicing it and hearing it named. When we point out kind actions, we help children understand what kindness looks like and how good it feels.

Try a kindness tree at home. A sheet of paper on the fridge works perfectly. Notice kindness in siblings, neighbors and yourselves, and watch your family's tree grow.
