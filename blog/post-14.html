Why We Let Children Get Messy

May 4, 2021

Parents sometimes laugh when they pick up a child with paint in their hair, sand in their shoes, and mud on their knees. We laugh too. But we also want families to know that the mess is not an accident. It is part of the plan.

Learning through the senses

Young children understand the world by touching, squeezing, pouring, and smearing. When a child pushes their hands through wet sand, they are learning about texture, weight, and cause and effect. Those experiences build the foundation for later learning in science and math.

Building strong hands

Playing with clay, play dough, and finger paint strengthens the small muscles in children's hands and fingers. Those same muscles will one day hold a pencil, button a coat, and tie a shoe.

Growing confidence

Messy play has no wrong answers. There is no "right" way to squish dough or mix colors. That freedom gives children a safe space to experiment, take risks, and feel successful.

Calming the body

Many children find sensory play deeply soothing. A child who is having a hard morning will often settle at the water table, slowly pouring from cup to cup. Teachers see it again and again.

Tips for families

  • Send your child in clothes that can get dirty, and keep a spare set in their cubby.
  • Try messy play at home in the bathtub, on a tarp, or outside.
  • Ask your child to describe what they made instead of what it is.
  • Focus on the process, not the finished product.

We know laundry is real, and we appreciate every family who sends their child ready to explore. Behind every stained shirt is a morning full of discovery, and we think that is a trade worth making.
