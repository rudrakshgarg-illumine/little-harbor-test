Why We Let Children Get Messy

April 15, 2021

If you have ever picked up your child with paint on their sleeve, sand in their shoes or a suspicious smudge of mud on their cheek, there is a good chance they had a wonderful day. At Little Harbor, we believe messy play is some of the most important learning children do.

Messy play builds the brain

When children squish clay, pour water or mix colors with their fingers, they are using all of their senses at once. That kind of hands-on exploration helps them understand how the world works: what sinks, what sticks, what spreads and what changes when you add a little more water.

It strengthens small hands

Scooping, squeezing, pinching and pouring build the small muscles children will later use to hold a pencil, zip a coat and cut with scissors. A morning at the sensory table is quietly getting little hands ready for big tasks.

It grows language

Ask a child to describe what they are touching, and you will hear a rush of new words: gooey, gritty, crumbly, slippery. Our teachers listen closely and add new vocabulary to the conversation as children play.

It builds confidence

There is no wrong way to explore a bin of wet sand. Open-ended play lets children experiment without worrying about getting the "right" answer, and that freedom helps them become braver learners.

A few tips for families

  • Send your child in clothes that are allowed to get dirty.
  • Keep a spare outfit in their cubby.
  • At home, try a bin of dry rice, a tub of water or a tray of shaving cream on the kitchen table.
  • Lay down a towel and let go of perfection.

We promise to keep encouraging the mess. We also promise to help with the cleanup, because tidying up together is part of learning too.
