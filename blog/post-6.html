Snow Days, Indoor Wonders

January 24, 2022

Chicago winters are long, and some days it is simply too cold for the playground. On those days, our teachers get creative about bringing the outdoors in, and families can do the same at home.

Bring the snow inside

One of our favorite winter activities is filling the sensory table with real snow. Children scoop it, pack it and watch it slowly melt into water. Add a few drops of food coloring, some cups and spoons, and suddenly it is a science lab. At home, a baking pan of snow on a towel works just as well.

Ice treasure hunts

Freeze small toys in containers of water overnight. The next day, give children warm water, droppers and salt to free their treasures. It takes patience, and the excitement when a plastic dinosaur finally breaks loose is worth every drip.

Indoor obstacle courses

Couch cushions, tape lines on the floor and a blanket tunnel can become a full-body adventure. Moving big muscles helps children regulate their energy on days when they cannot run outside. In our classrooms, teachers add songs and silly challenges, like "crawl like a bear" or "tiptoe like a fox."

Cozy story corners

Winter is the perfect season for building a reading fort with pillows and a flashlight. Look for books about snow, hibernation and animal tracks, and talk about where animals go when it is cold.

Window watching

Some of the best winter learning happens at the window. Children notice birds, frost patterns, footprints and snowplows. Ask your child what they see and what they wonder about it.

When the weather does allow it, bundle up and get outside, even for a few minutes. Fresh air, crunchy snow and rosy cheeks are part of a Chicago childhood, and we love every snowy minute.
