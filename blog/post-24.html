Helping Your Child Through Big Feelings

February 9, 2021

Tears over a broken cracker. Stomping feet when it's time to leave the park. A shout of "I'm so mad!" Big feelings are a normal, healthy part of early childhood. Children feel emotions intensely, but they're still learning how to understand and manage them.

Here are a few approaches our teachers use every day that you can try at home.

Name the Feeling

When a child is upset, calmly put words to what you see: "You're frustrated because the tower fell down." Naming emotions helps children recognize them and lets them know they've been understood.

Stay Calm and Close

Your calm presence is powerful. Getting down to your child's level, speaking softly, and simply staying nearby tells them they're safe, even when their feelings feel too big.

Validate, Then Guide

All feelings are okay, but not all actions are. You might say, "It's okay to feel angry. It's not okay to hit. Let's stomp our feet instead." This acknowledges the emotion while setting a clear, kind limit.

Teach Calming Tools

When children are calm, practice strategies they can use later:

  • Balloon breaths: Breathe in slowly to fill up like a balloon, then let the air out.
  • Squeeze and release: Make tight fists, then let them go.
  • A cozy spot: A quiet corner with a pillow or favorite book.
  • Counting: Count slowly to five together.

Read About Feelings

Picture books about emotions open the door to great conversations. Ask, "How do you think this character feels? What could help them?"

Be Patient With Yourself, Too

No parent handles every moment perfectly. If you lose your cool, it's okay to repair: "I yelled, and I'm sorry. Let's try again." That teaches children something important as well.
