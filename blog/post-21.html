Five Small Ways to Make Mornings Less Rushed

March 4, 2019

If your mornings feel like a race you never signed up for, you are in good company. Almost every family we talk to at Little Harbor has a story about a missing shoe, a sudden meltdown over the "wrong" cup, or a coat that refuses to go on. The good news is that small changes can make a real difference.

Start the Night Before

Lay out clothes, pack bags, and set shoes by the door before bedtime. When children help choose between two outfits the night before, there is far less negotiation at sunrise.

Try a Picture Routine

Young children love knowing what comes next. A simple chart with drawings or photos of each step (wake up, potty, get dressed, eat breakfast, brush teeth, shoes on) lets your child take ownership. Our teachers use picture schedules in the classroom every day, and families tell us they work just as well at home.

Build in Buffer Time

Children move at their own pace, and that pace is rarely fast. Adding a cushion to your morning means a dawdle doesn't turn into a crisis.

Offer Real Choices

  • "Do you want to put on your socks or your shirt first?"
  • "Would you like to walk to the car or hop like a bunny?"
  • "Should we sing a song or tell a story while we drive?"

Choices give children a sense of control, which often means less resistance.

Keep Goodbyes Short and Loving

A consistent goodbye ritual, like a hug, a high five, and a "see you after nap," helps children feel secure. Lingering can make separation harder. Trust that our teachers are ready to welcome your child warmly and help them settle into the day.

Mornings won't ever be perfect, and that's okay. What matters most is that your child starts the day feeling loved. Have a morning tip of your own? Share it with your child's teacher. We love passing good ideas along to other families.
