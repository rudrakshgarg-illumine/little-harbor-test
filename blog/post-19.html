Getting Ready for Kindergarten Without the Stress

April 15, 2025

As spring arrives, many families start thinking about the big move to kindergarten. It is natural to wonder whether your child is ready. The good news is that readiness is about much more than knowing letters and numbers, and much of it grows through everyday play.

Independence matters

Being able to open a lunch container, put on a coat, use the bathroom independently, and wash hands go a long way toward a confident first day. Give your child chances to practice these skills at home, even when it would be faster to do it yourself.

Social skills are key

Kindergarten teachers often say that taking turns, asking for help, and getting along with others are among the most important skills a child can bring. Play dates, board games, and family activities all build these abilities.

Listening and following directions

Simple games like Simon Says or "Can you get your shoes and then your hat?" help children practice listening and remembering steps.

Early literacy and math, the playful way

Read together, point out letters on signs, count stairs, and sort laundry by color. These everyday moments build strong foundations without worksheets or pressure.

Talk about the change

Read books about starting school, walk or drive past the new school, and share happy memories from your own school days. Keep your tone positive and excited.

How our centers help

In our preschool classrooms, teachers build these skills into each day through routines, small-group activities, and lots of play. As children approach the transition, teachers talk with them about what kindergarten might be like and celebrate how much they have grown.

If you have questions about your child's progress, please talk with your child's teacher. We are here to partner with you. Every child develops at their own pace, and with your support, they will step into kindergarten ready to shine.
