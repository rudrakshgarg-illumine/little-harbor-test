The Great Pumpkin Investigation

October 28, 2020

Every fall, pumpkins arrive in our classrooms, and every fall they turn our preschoolers into scientists. This year, one class decided to find out everything they possibly could about one very large, very lumpy orange pumpkin.

It began with a question during morning circle: "Is it heavier than me?" That sparked an entire week of investigation. The children took turns trying to lift it, guessed its weight, and then compared it with backpacks, blocks and a stuffed bear. The pumpkin won every contest except one, against a full bin of wooden blocks, which caused a great deal of cheering.

Next came measuring. Using lengths of yarn, the children wrapped the pumpkin around its middle and compared their yarn to their own waists, arms and even a teacher's leg. There was a lot of giggling, and a lot of real thinking about longer, shorter and "almost the same."

Then came the moment everyone had been waiting for: opening it up. Some children dove right in with both hands. Others preferred a spoon, and a few chose to watch from a safe distance while describing the smell. "It smells like the inside of a cucumber," one child decided. Words like slimy, stringy, squishy and cold filled the room.

The class counted seeds in groups of ten, which took a long time and required several snack breaks. Afterward, they rinsed and dried some of the seeds to plant in the spring, and they drew predictions about what might grow.

What we love about projects like this is that they come from the children's own curiosity. One simple question grew into math, science, language, teamwork and plenty of laughter.

Try it at home: pick up a small pumpkin and ask your child what they wonder about it. You may be surprised where the conversation leads.
