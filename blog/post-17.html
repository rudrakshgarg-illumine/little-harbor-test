The Power of Reading Aloud, Even in Short Bursts

March 2, 2023

Families often ask us what they can do at home to support learning. Our answer is almost always the same: read together. Reading aloud is one of the simplest and most powerful gifts you can give a young child.

It does not have to be long

Busy evenings are real. The good news is that even a few minutes with a book makes a difference. Consistency matters more than length. A short story every night builds a habit your child will look forward to.

Reread the favorites

If you have read the same book fifty times, you are doing it right. Repetition helps children learn new words, predict what comes next, and feel confident. Soon they will be "reading" along with you from memory, which is an important early literacy step.

Talk about the pictures

You do not need to read every word. Point to the illustrations and ask questions: "What do you see? What do you think will happen?" These conversations build vocabulary and thinking skills.

Let your child lead

Let your child choose the book, turn the pages, and decide when the story is done. When children feel in charge, they stay engaged.

Use silly voices

Do not be shy. Growly bears, squeaky mice, and dramatic pauses make stories come alive. Children remember the stories that made them laugh.

Reading in our centers

In our classrooms, books are everywhere: in cozy reading corners, in the block area, even near the sensory table. Teachers read with children throughout the day, in large groups and one-on-one. We love seeing a child carry a book to a teacher's lap and settle in.

This month, as schools everywhere celebrate reading, we encourage you to find a small, cozy moment with your child and a good book. Those minutes add up to a lifelong love of stories.
