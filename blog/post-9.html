Growing a Garden, Growing Patience

May 20, 2025

This spring, several of our classrooms became gardeners. With small pots, bags of soil and a lot of enthusiasm, children planted seeds and then faced the hardest part of gardening: waiting.

On planting day, the excitement was enormous. Children scooped soil, poked holes with their fingers and dropped in seeds while counting carefully. A few seeds ended up in the wrong pot, and a fair amount of soil ended up on the floor. That was all part of the fun.

Then came the waiting. Every morning, children checked their pots. "Nothing yet," they reported, sometimes with great disappointment. Teachers used those moments to talk about what seeds need: water, sunlight, time and care. The children took turns watering and decided together whether the soil felt dry or damp.

When the first tiny green sprouts appeared, the cheering could be heard down the hall. Children grabbed magnifying glasses, drew pictures in their journals and measured the sprouts with paper strips each week. Some plants grew tall quickly. Others took their time. One never came up at all, which led to a thoughtful conversation about how not everything grows the way we expect.

Gardening teaches so much more than science. Children practice patience, responsibility and gentle hands. They learn to care for something outside themselves, and they feel proud watching it grow.

Try it at home

  • Plant beans in a clear cup with a damp paper towel so children can see roots grow.
  • Let your child be in charge of watering.
  • Keep a simple drawing journal of changes.
  • Visit a local garden or park and notice what is blooming.

Whether you have a backyard or a single sunny windowsill, a little garden can bring a lot of wonder into your child's world.
