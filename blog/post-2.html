Five Small Ways to Make Mornings Calmer

September 9, 2019

If your mornings feel like a race between breakfast, shoes and the front door, you are in very good company. At drop-off, we see plenty of parents arriving with coffee in one hand, a lunch bag in the other and a child who has decided today is the day to wear rain boots in the sunshine. Here are a few ideas our teachers and families have found helpful.

1. Move decisions to the night before

Children love choices, but too many in the morning can stall everything. Try letting your child pick between two outfits before bed and laying them out together. It becomes a cozy ritual instead of a morning debate.

2. Make a picture schedule

A simple chart with drawings or photos of each step, such as getting dressed, eating breakfast, brushing teeth and putting on shoes, helps young children feel capable. In our classrooms, visual schedules give children a sense of control, and they work beautifully at home too.

3. Build in a few extra minutes

Children rarely move at grown-up speed. A small cushion of time leaves room for a dandelion on the sidewalk or a very important question about worms.

4. Keep goodbyes short and loving

A consistent goodbye, like a hug, a high five and "I'll see you after snack and play," helps children know what to expect. Lingering can sometimes make separation harder. Our teachers are always ready to help with the handoff.

5. Name the feelings

When a morning goes sideways, try saying what you see: "You feel frustrated because the sock feels bumpy." Feeling understood often helps children move forward faster than hurrying ever does.

No morning routine is perfect, and some days will simply be rough. That is okay. What children remember most is the warmth along the way, not whether the shoes matched.
