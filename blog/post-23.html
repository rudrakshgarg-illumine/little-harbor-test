Fall Leaf Fun: Simple Activities to Try at Home

October 15, 2020

Autumn around Chicago is a gift for little explorers. Crunchy leaves, cool air, and changing colors offer endless chances to play and learn. You don't need special supplies, just a backyard, a park, or a neighborhood sidewalk.

Leaf Hunt

Give your child a bag and a challenge: find a red leaf, a yellow leaf, a brown leaf, a big leaf, and a tiny leaf. This simple game builds vocabulary, color recognition, and comparison skills.

Sort and Count

Once you're home, spread your leaves on the table. Ask your child to sort them by color, size, or shape. Then count each pile. Which group has the most? Which has the fewest? These are the same early math ideas our teachers explore in the classroom.

Leaf Rubbings

Place a leaf under a sheet of paper and rub the side of an unwrapped crayon over it. Watch the veins and edges appear like magic. Children are always delighted, and it's a wonderful way to strengthen the hand muscles that help with writing later on.

Jump in the Pile

Don't skip the most important activity of all. Raking leaves together and jumping into the pile is joyful, active play. It builds gross motor skills, and it builds memories too.

Talk About Change

Fall is a natural time to talk about how things change. Try questions like:

  • "Why do you think the leaves are changing color?"
  • "What do you notice about the trees now?"
  • "How is today different from summer?"

There are no wrong answers. The goal is to get your child noticing, wondering, and talking.
