Five Ways to Make Goodbyes Easier at Drop-Off

September 9, 2019

If your child clings to your leg at the classroom door, you are in very good company. Separation feelings are a normal, healthy part of growing up, and they tell us something lovely: your child is deeply attached to you. Still, mornings go more smoothly with a little planning. Here are five ideas our teachers share with families every fall.

1. Keep the goodbye short and warm

A long, drawn-out farewell can make worries grow. Give a hug, say a clear goodbye, and head out with a smile. Your calm confidence tells your child that this is a safe place.

2. Create a special ritual

Some families do a "kiss in the palm" to hold all day. Others wave from the same window every morning. A predictable ritual gives children something to count on.

3. Never sneak away

It is tempting to slip out while your child is busy, but disappearing can make children more anxious the next day. Always say goodbye, even if there are a few tears.

4. Talk about the day ahead

On the drive in, mention something your child can look forward to: the block corner, a favorite friend, the classroom fish. Naming a happy moment helps little ones step into the room with purpose.

5. Trust the teachers

Our teachers have comforted many tearful children, and they are skilled at gently guiding a child toward an activity. Most of the time, tears dry within minutes of a parent leaving. If you are ever wondering how the morning went, just ask. We are always glad to share.

Remember, hard goodbyes do not mean something is wrong. They are one small step in learning that people we love leave and always come back. With patience and consistency, those wobbly mornings turn into confident waves, usually sooner than you expect.
