Why Play Is Serious Learning

September 6, 2022

Families sometimes ask us, "Do they just play all day?" It's a fair question. When you walk into one of our classrooms, you'll likely see children building with blocks, pretending to run a restaurant, or splashing at the water table. What you're actually seeing is learning in action.

Blocks Build More Than Towers

When children stack blocks, they're testing balance, comparing sizes, and solving problems. When the tower falls, they figure out why and try again. That's early engineering, math, and persistence, all in one activity.

Pretend Play Builds Language and Empathy

In the dramatic play corner, a child might be a doctor one minute and a chef the next. Pretend play lets children practice new vocabulary, take turns, negotiate roles, and imagine how others feel. "You be the customer, I'll be the cook" is a lesson in cooperation.

Sensory Play Builds Focus

Sand, water, playdough, and other sensory materials help children concentrate, explore cause and effect, and strengthen small hand muscles they'll later use to hold a pencil.

Outdoor Play Builds Bodies and Confidence

Running, climbing, and balancing develop coordination and strength. Children also learn to assess risk and celebrate their own accomplishments: "I made it to the top!"

The Teacher's Role

Play at Little Harbor isn't random. Our teachers thoughtfully set up materials, observe children closely, and step in with questions that stretch thinking:

  • "What do you think will happen if...?"
  • "How could you make it taller?"
  • "How can you and your friend share that?"

Supporting Play at Home

You don't need fancy toys. Cardboard boxes, pots and pans, scarves, and crayons can fuel hours of creative play. The most valuable thing you can offer is time, and maybe joining in now and then.

So yes, our children play all day. And they're learning every minute of it.
