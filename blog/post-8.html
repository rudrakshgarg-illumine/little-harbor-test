Talking With Your Child About Big Feelings

June 6, 2024

Young children feel things deeply. A broken cracker can feel like a disaster, and saying goodbye to a friend at the end of the day can bring real tears. These moments can be hard for grown-ups too. Here are some approaches our teachers use every day that families can bring home.

Stay close and calm

When a child is upset, they borrow calm from the adults around them. Getting down to their level, using a soft voice and simply being nearby can help more than any words.

Name what you see

Try saying, "You are really angry that playtime is over." Naming feelings helps children understand what is happening inside them, and it shows them that their feelings make sense.

Accept the feeling, guide the behavior

All feelings are okay, but not all actions are. You might say, "It is okay to be mad. I won't let you hit. You can stomp your feet or squeeze this pillow." Clear, gentle limits help children feel safe.

Wait to teach

In the middle of a meltdown, children cannot learn new lessons. Once they are calm, talk about what happened and what they might try next time.

Read books about feelings

Stories give children words and pictures for emotions. Pause while reading to ask, "How do you think he feels? Have you ever felt that way?"

Take care of yourself too

Parenting through big feelings is tiring. It is okay to take a breath, step back for a moment or ask for help. Our teachers are always happy to talk about what works in the classroom and partner with you at home.

Every tearful moment is also a chance to learn. With patience and practice, children grow the skills to handle big feelings, one small moment at a time.
