Summer Water Play: Splashing, Pouring and Learning

July 14, 2026

When summer heat settles over Chicagoland, nothing brings out bigger smiles than water play. Across our centers, splash days, water tables, and sprinkler fun have become favorite parts of the season, and they are full of learning too.

Little scientists at work

Water is a perfect science material. Children discover which objects sink and which float, how water moves through funnels and tubes, and what happens when they pour from a big cup into a small one. Teachers ask questions like "What do you think will happen?" and let children test their ideas.

Math in every cup

Full, empty, more, less, half: these early math words come up naturally at the water table. Counting scoops and comparing containers helps children build number sense without even realizing it.

Teamwork and turns

Sharing buckets and passing the watering can gives children plenty of practice with cooperation. We often see children invent games together, like building a "river" with tubes or running a pretend car wash for toy trucks.

Safety always comes first

Water play in our classrooms is always closely supervised by teachers. We also talk with children about simple safety habits, like walking on wet surfaces and drinking water to stay cool.

Easy water play at home

  • Fill a bin with water and add cups, spoons, and sponges.
  • "Paint" the sidewalk or fence with a brush and plain water.
  • Wash toy dishes or plastic animals with a little soap.
  • Freeze small toys in ice and let your child rescue them.

Please remember to send your child with any summer items your center has requested, so they are ready for water fun. We hope your family enjoys every splash of this season. Summer goes by fast, and these joyful, soggy moments are some of the best memories of early childhood.
