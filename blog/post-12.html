The Great Pumpkin Investigation

October 28, 2019

Every October, pumpkins show up in our classrooms, and every October our children prove they are natural scientists. This year one of our preschool classrooms turned a single, very large pumpkin into a two-week investigation, and we had to share the story.

It started with a simple question from a child during morning circle: "Is it heavy or light inside?" The teacher did not answer. Instead, she asked the class how they could find out. Hands shot up. Someone suggested lifting it. Someone else suggested sitting on it. (We politely passed on that one.)

Making predictions

The children drew pictures of what they thought was inside. We saw seeds, of course, but also predictions of water, candy, and in one memorable drawing, a tiny orange house. Every idea went up on the wall with the child's name beside it.

Opening it up

On carving day, children took turns scooping with big spoons. The words started flowing: slimy, stringy, cold, squishy, gross, awesome. Teachers wrote every word on chart paper, building a pumpkin vocabulary wall that stayed up for weeks.

Counting the seeds

Next came the counting. Children sorted seeds into groups of ten on paper plates, then counted the plates together. The final number got a big cheer, and several children insisted on counting again "just to be sure."

What happened next

The class planted a few seeds in cups by the window, roasted some for a tasting, and placed the carved pumpkin outside to watch it change. Over the next days, they noticed it soften and sag, which led to a great conversation about how things decompose and return to the earth.

One pumpkin, many lessons: science, math, language, and a whole lot of joy. It is a perfect reminder that the best learning often begins with a child's own question.
