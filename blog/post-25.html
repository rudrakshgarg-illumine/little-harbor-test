Our Classroom Garden Grows Up

June 17, 2022

There's something special about watching a child pull a carrot out of the dirt for the very first time. This spring, children across our Little Harbor centers got their hands dirty, and the results have been delicious.

From Seed to Sprout

It started indoors with paper cups, potting soil, and a lot of patience. Children planted seeds, labeled their cups, and checked them every day. When the first green shoots appeared, the excitement was contagious. "It's alive!" became the most common phrase in the room.

Teachers used the sprouts to talk about what plants need: sunlight, water, and soil. Children took turns as "garden helpers," watering carefully and recording changes in their own drawings.

Moving Outside

Once the weather warmed, the seedlings moved into outdoor garden spaces. Children helped dig, plant, and water. They discovered worms, roly-polies, and the occasional curious bird. Every visit outdoors became a chance to observe and ask questions.

What Children Learned

  • Science: Life cycles, weather, and how living things grow.
  • Math: Measuring plant height, counting seeds, and comparing sizes.
  • Language: New words like "sprout," "roots," "harvest," and "compost."
  • Responsibility: Caring for something that depends on them.

The Best Part: Tasting

When harvest time came, children proudly washed and tasted what they had grown. We saw some brave first bites of vegetables that would usually be pushed to the side of a plate. It turns out that food tastes better when you grew it yourself.

Try It at Home

You don't need a big yard. A sunny windowsill and a pot of herbs or beans can spark the same wonder. Let your child do the watering, and watch their pride grow along with the plant.

We're so grateful to the families who sent in seeds, gloves, and gardening tools. Our gardens truly were a community effort.
