The Great Worm Rescue: A Rainy Day Story

April 22, 2019

Some of the best lessons in early childhood aren't planned at all. They wiggle up out of the ground after a spring rain.

One April morning, a group of our preschoolers headed outside after a storm and discovered the sidewalk dotted with earthworms. At first there were a few squeals. Then one child crouched down, looked closely, and announced, "They're lost. We have to help them get home."

And just like that, the Great Worm Rescue began.

Curiosity Leads the Way

Our teacher grabbed a few small cups and some leaves, and the children got to work gently scooping worms back into the grass. Along the way, the questions came fast:

  • "Why do worms come out when it rains?"
  • "Do worms have faces?"
  • "Which end is the front?"
  • "Do they have families?"

Rather than answering everything on the spot, our teacher wrote the questions down. Back inside, the class turned them into a mini research project with picture books, magnifying glasses, and a lot of careful observation.

Learning That Sticks

Over the next week, the children learned that worms help keep soil healthy, that they breathe through their skin, and that they really do prefer to stay out of the sun. They drew worm diagrams, counted segments, and even acted out "worm wiggles" during movement time.

What we loved most, though, was the kindness. Children who had been nervous at first became the most careful rescuers. Several reminded friends to "use gentle hands" and "watch your feet."

Why We Follow Their Lead

At Little Harbor, we believe children learn best when their own questions guide the way. A rainy sidewalk became a lesson in science, language, math, and empathy, all because a child noticed something and cared.

Next time it rains, take a walk with your child. You never know who might need rescuing.
