Getting Ready for Kindergarten: It's Not Just ABCs

March 12, 2025

As spring arrives, many of our families start thinking about the big step ahead: kindergarten. It's natural to wonder whether your child is "ready." While letters and numbers matter, the skills that help children thrive in kindergarten go far beyond flashcards.

Independence

Kindergarten asks children to do more on their own. Practice skills like:

  • Putting on and zipping a coat
  • Opening lunch containers
  • Using the bathroom and washing hands independently
  • Keeping track of their own belongings

Every small "I did it myself!" builds confidence.

Social Skills

Being able to take turns, share, ask for help, and work through disagreements makes a big difference. In our pre-K classrooms, teachers spend a lot of time helping children practice these skills through group games, projects, and everyday play.

Listening and Following Directions

At home, try giving two- or three-step directions: "Please put your shoes by the door, then wash your hands." Games like "Simon Says" are a fun way to practice listening, too.

A Love of Books

Reading together every day is one of the best things you can do. Talk about the pictures, ask what might happen next, and let your child "read" to you by retelling stories. Children who love books come to school eager to learn.

Fine Motor Skills

Cutting with scissors, drawing, playing with playdough, and stringing beads all strengthen the hand muscles needed for writing.

Talking About the Change

Speak positively about kindergarten. Read books about starting school, drive past the new building, and invite your child to share what they're excited or nervous about. Their feelings are valid, and talking helps.

Remember, every child develops at their own pace. If you have questions about your child's readiness, our teachers are always glad to share what they've observed and offer ideas.
