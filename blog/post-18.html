A Day of Kindness: Our Classroom Thank-You Project

November 20, 2023

This November, children in one of our preschool classrooms asked a big question: "Who helps us?" What followed was a heartwarming project that reached far beyond the classroom walls.

Making a list

The teacher invited children to think about all the people who make their days better. The list grew quickly: parents, grandparents, the cook, the people who clean the building, the mail carrier, firefighters, bus drivers, and, according to one child, "the dog next door who is always happy to see me."

Creating thank-you cards

Over several days, children drew pictures and dictated messages for the people on their list. Teachers wrote down each child's exact words. Some notes were simple, like "Thank you for the food." Others were wonderfully specific: "Thank you for fixing the sink so the water does not go everywhere."

Delivering the cards

Children personally handed cards to the staff members in their building. The looks on those faces were priceless. Other cards went home for families to deliver to neighbors and community helpers.

What children learned

Gratitude can feel like an abstract idea for young children, but this project made it concrete. Children practiced noticing the work others do, using their words to express appreciation, and seeing how their kindness made someone else smile.

Try it at home

  • At dinner, take turns naming one person who helped you today.
  • Draw a picture together for a grandparent or friend.
  • Let your child say thank you to a cashier or librarian.
  • Talk about how it feels when someone thanks you.
