Programs at Little Harbor Oak Park

At our Lake Street center, children ages 6 weeks to 5 years learn and grow through five programs. Each one builds on the last, so children move forward with confidence while staying in a community they already know.

Infant

Our Infant program welcomes babies as young as 6 weeks. In this room, everything begins with relationships. Teachers follow each baby's own rhythms for feeding, sleeping, and play, and they respond with patience and warmth. Days are filled with cuddles, songs, gentle conversation, and safe space to stretch, roll, and reach. We know that leaving your baby is a big step, so we work closely with families to make each transition feel as smooth and reassuring as possible.

Toddler

Toddlers are explorers, and our Toddler program gives them room to explore safely. Children practice new movements, discover how things work, and begin finding words for what they see and feel. Teachers support budding independence with simple routines and lots of encouragement, celebrating the small victories that matter so much at this age, like a first word or a first try at drinking from a cup.

Twos

Two-year-olds have big ideas and big feelings, and our Twos program honors both. Children build language, practice sharing and taking turns, and gain self-help skills that help them feel capable. Teachers guide children through emotions with calm, kind support, helping them learn to name their feelings and work through them. Play is at the center of the day, with plenty of chances to create, move, and pretend.

Preschool

In Preschool, curiosity really takes off. Children ask questions, test ideas, and work together on projects that spark their interests. Teachers weave early literacy, math, and science into hands-on play, while also helping children grow friendships and learn how to be part of a classroom community. Preschoolers leave each day with stories to tell about what they discovered.

Pre-K

Our Pre-K program prepares children for the step into kindergarten. Children strengthen the skills they will need next, including listening, following multi-step directions, recognizing letters and numbers, and expressing their ideas clearly. Just as important, they build confidence, independence, and a love of learning. By the end of Pre-K, our oldest children are ready to walk into kindergarten feeling proud and prepared.
