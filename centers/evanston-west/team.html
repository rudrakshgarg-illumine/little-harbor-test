Little Harbor Early Learning

Our Team at Evanston West

A school is only as good as the people in it. At Little Harbor's Evanston West center on Dodge Avenue, our team shares one simple goal: every child who walks through our doors should feel known, safe, and excited to learn.

Meet Our Director, Kevin Osei

Kevin Osei leads Evanston West, and families quickly get to know him as a familiar, friendly face at drop-off and pick-up. Kevin works alongside our teachers to keep classrooms running smoothly, supports staff as they plan meaningful experiences for children, and makes sure families always have someone to talk to. If you have a question about your child's day, a concern you'd like to talk through, or a story you want to share, Kevin's door is open.

Under Kevin's leadership, the outdoor nature classroom has become a central part of daily life at Evanston West. He believes children learn deeply when they have time to explore, get a little muddy, and follow their own curiosity.

Our Teachers

Our teachers are people who genuinely enjoy young children. They get down on the floor to build block towers, they kneel to look closely at a beetle a child has found, and they know that a hard goodbye at the door sometimes just needs a patient hug and a favorite book.

Because we serve children ages 2 to 5 years, our teachers focus on the toddler and preschool years. They understand how quickly children change during this time, from a two-year-old learning to say "my turn" to a Pre-K student getting ready for kindergarten. They plan with that growth in mind across our Twos, Preschool, and Pre-K programs.

Our teachers also love being outside. They help children explore the outdoor nature classroom with curiosity and care, turning everyday discoveries into chances to learn.

Partners With Families

We see parents and caregivers as our partners. Our team takes time to share what children are learning and how they're growing, and we want to hear from you too. You know your child best, and that knowledge helps us teach well.
